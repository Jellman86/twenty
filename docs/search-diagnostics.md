# Candidate-family uncertainty diagnostics

Implemented on `dev`, 15 October 2026. This is a retrospective analysis of recorded development returns. It does not nominate a champion, open the reserved holdout or authorise execution. The current bundled data remain synthetic.

## Research basis

Politis & Romano (1994), **The Stationary Bootstrap**, motivates resampling consecutive observations with geometrically distributed block lengths. The implementation starts at a uniformly chosen month, then either continues to the next month with circular wraparound or restarts uniformly with probability `1 / expectedBlockMonths`. A shared index path is applied to the entire multivariate return vector. Full algorithm/proposition passages on pages 1303–1305 were inspected in the [published-paper scan](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~behansen/718/Politis%20Romano.pdf); [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870). Stationarity and sufficient history remain assumptions, not properties established by this software.

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu's **Probability of Backtest Overfitting** motivates retaining complete candidate return streams and examining selection across alternatives. [Author manuscript](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf). Bailey & López de Prado's **Deflated Sharpe Ratio** additionally requires return moments and assumptions about independent trials; the raw registry count is not an effective independent-trial count. [Author manuscript, including Appendix 3](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

The bootstrap below is an explicit Twenty diagnostic using stationary resampling and a joint maximum-deviation statistic; it is not a replication of White's Reality Check. Since 18 October 2026 the same recorded family also carries a scoped CSCV-PBO estimate and a deflated-Sharpe estimate, implemented from the two manuscripts above under declared assumptions (next sections). Neither can correct trials that are missing from the recorded family.

## Inputs and family

Select a completed development run in the Growth lab and request **Analyse the recorded candidate family**. The server snapshots all locally registered attempts, including cancelled/failed ones. The caller cannot supply a shortlist. All completed runs with the anchor's exact dataset, core assembly hash, monthly contribution, cost schedule, allocation policy and reserved-window length enter the family. Other attempts remain listed with exclusion reasons and hashes; they have not disappeared from the research search. Different model hypotheses, training windows, refit settings, concentrations and regularisation values can enter the same family.

Every included report is read from its immutable hashed artifact and checked against its run contract. The analysis uses the intersection of their recorded development calendar windows, requires at least 24 consecutive months, and never pads missing returns. Each account retains its original pre-intersection holdings/cash history; this is not a new reset-account experiment. There is no reserved-price read or refit. Comparing runs that previously used different holdout lengths does not restore unseen data: they are excluded from this family and remain visible.

Imported provenance declarations and exact hashes establish integrity, not trustworthy point-in-time coverage. A changed metadata file creates another dataset identity; searches across such identities, other engine versions, output roots or manual data inspection are outside these bands. No entire-programme multiplicity claim is made.

## Output

For every candidate versus benchmark, momentum, equal targets and zero-interest cash, compute `12 × mean(candidate monthly net return − comparator monthly net return)`. These are annualised arithmetic mean differences in percentage points. They are not compound growth rates, risk-free-adjusted Sharpe ratios, prediction scores or probabilities of reaching a wealth target.

For each fixed expected block length of 3, 6 and 12 months, generate 2,000 joint resamples with seed 212. The pointwise range is the 2.5th–97.5th percentile of each resampled mean. To account for comparing the included family, each replicate also records the maximum absolute centred mean deviation across **all candidates and all four comparators**. Its 95th percentile supplies a common radius around each observed mean. This is an approximate simultaneous band within that block setting, not simultaneous coverage across the three sensitivities. Quantiles use linear interpolation; the recorded method version, worker commit and diagnostic assembly hash identify the implementation/runtime build.

Repeated identical return streams do not become independent observations: they use the same resample indices and do not enlarge the maximum. Incomplete trials cannot supply return paths; their presence is disclosed and prevents treating the scoped family as the whole programme. A range wholly above zero is not an automatic promotion criterion.

Short histories can contain very few expected blocks. Weak dependence, stable distributions, finite moments and the bootstrap approximation may all be inappropriate for concentrated speculative returns. These ranges hold fitted models and the selected universe fixed; they omit model-estimation, provider, survivorship, delisting, execution and structural-regime uncertainty. Use all three sensitivities, cost stress and new prospective evidence; do not pick the most flattering band.

The reported expected block starts are `1 + (months − 1) / expectedBlockMonths`: the initial block plus expected restarts. This is not an effective independent sample size.

## Selection estimates: CSCV PBO and deflated Sharpe

Added 18 October 2026 (`stationary-active-mean-v2`), advancing [#7](https://github.com/Jellman86/twenty/issues/7). Both statistics run on the same calendar-aligned common window as the bootstrap, over candidate **net monthly period returns** (not active differences). Sharpe means mean ÷ sample standard deviation against Twenty's zero-interest cash; monthly units unless labelled annualised (×√12). Deterministic; no resampling. Selection requires at least two candidates and 48 common months; otherwise the section records why it is unavailable instead of failing the bootstrap diagnostic.

**CSCV PBO (P21, Algorithm 2.3).** The window is partitioned into 16 contiguous blocks (first `months mod 16` blocks one month longer). All 12,870 ways to choose 8 blocks as in-sample are enumerated. The in-sample Sharpe winner's out-of-sample fractional rank (average ranks for ties, 1 = worst) gives ω = rank/(N+1) and logit λ = ln(ω/(1−ω)). PBO is the fraction of splits with λ < 0 — the winner below the family median out-of-sample. Also reported: median logit, median in/out-of-sample winner Sharpe, and the fraction of splits whose winner has negative out-of-sample Sharpe. In-sample ties break by ordinal run id. A zero-variance half has Sharpe 0 (mean zero) or a declared ±10^15 limit convention (nonzero mean), so ranking stays defined. Performance-degradation slopes and stochastic-dominance analysis from the paper are not implemented.

**Deflated Sharpe (P22, eq. 2).** The family's full-window Sharpe winner is deflated against the expected maximum Sharpe under zero skill, SR* = σ̂·[(1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))], where σ̂ is the standard deviation of candidate Sharpes across the family and γ is Euler–Mascheroni. The extreme-value approximation is floored at zero: for tiny trial counts it goes negative, which would inflate rather than deflate. DSR = Φ((SR−SR*)√(T−1) / √(1 − skew·SR + ((kurt−1)/4)·SR²)) with the winner's sample skewness and raw kurtosis (normal = 3). PSR against zero is reported undeflated for contrast. Trial counts are **declared sensitivities**, following Appendix A.3: family size as-if-independent; correlation-adjusted N̂ = ρ̄ + (1−ρ̄)M from the average pairwise return correlation (clamped to [0,1]); and, when larger, every locally registered attempt in the snapshot as-if-independent. The paper itself warns the average-correlation estimate can be overfit on short samples; no count is measured truth. Normal CDF/inverse use an incomplete-gamma/Acklam implementation pinned to independent reference values within 1e-9, and the whole engine was cross-checked against an independent Python implementation to machine precision on identical authored inputs (committed as `scripts/crosscheck_selection.py` so the agreement stays reproducible).

Neither number is a whole-programme correction. Searches across other datasets, engine versions, workspaces or manual inspection remain outside both, and a flattering PBO or DSR is not a promotion criterion.

## Persistence and bounds

`POST /api/research/diagnostics` takes `{ "requestId": "UUID", "anchorRunId": "completed-run-id" }`. Requests require the existing same-origin header. A repeated request returns the same attempt; conflicting anchor reuse fails. `GET /api/research/diagnostics` lists the latest 20 summaries and total count; `GET /api/research/diagnostics/{id}` returns the full snapshot/result. No credentials or private provider payloads are accepted by this API.

The separate SQLite journal persists admitted attempts before analysis, including failures, cancellations and interrupted requests. Restart marks a running analysis interrupted; it is not silently rerun. Successful payloads include dataset/synthetic status, register-snapshot time, all local trial/config/report/engine hashes, analysis code identity and every sensitivity. Stored payload hashes detect corruption. A repeated analysis is another visible attempt; it is not a new independent market history.

Bounds: one diagnostic at a time, two-minute deadline including report loading, at most 64 compatible candidates, 200 attempts, 1 MiB per journal payload and 32 MiB total journal payloads. Excess candidates fail rather than silently trimming the family. Reports are extracted one at a time. Analysis and an explicitly requested training job can share the container's single CPU; neither runs periodically or requires a GPU.

The separate [registered study workflow](registered-studies.md) now declares a bounded cost-stress family, freezes a choice and spends one final pair. Genuine data acceptance, whole-programme search correction and prospective promotion remain open in [issue 7](https://github.com/Jellman86/twenty/issues/7). The standalone retrospective diagnostic does not itself perform those actions; existing broker requirements remain unchanged.

## Changing-universe families and complete disclosure (20 October 2026)

The same diagnostic request now accepts a completed changing-universe run as its anchor. The workbench exposes this action after opening a successful universe report. New analyses snapshot both model and universe registers together under the research admission/state lock. Every attempt in both registers is retained with its status, hashes and inclusion/exclusion reason; the caller still cannot supply a shortlist. Existing diagnostic snapshots remain unchanged.

For a universe family, each candidate is the recorded **momentum baseline** under one configuration. Equal-weight and zero-interest cash are its two actual comparators. `stationary-universe-active-mean-v1` uses the same joint bootstrap/selection kernel with that fixed pair; no external benchmark or model-return stream is invented. The ordinary four-comparator method retains its existing mathematical behaviour and method label. Selection estimates retain the same minimum-history and family-size requirements. Every registered local attempt, including excluded attempts from the other engine, enters only the explicitly labelled registered-count-as-independent sensitivity; that count is not an estimated number of independent trials or a whole-programme correction.

Compatible universe runs must match exact dataset digest, universe id, scientific identity scheme/hash, panel digest, decision instants, maximum price age, monthly contribution and cost schedule. Momentum concentration settings may differ. Incomplete attempts, other datasets/universes/code/panels and different account/grid/price policies remain disclosed. The anchor must retain its original hash-verified request; missing legacy evidence is refused instead of reconstructed. For universe rows, the existing `configSha256` field contains the retained semantic **request** digest, which includes the decision grid, staleness bound, costs and configuration; `engineSha256` contains the recorded scientific digest. `researchEngine: changing-universe` distinguishes their interpretation from ordinary model rows.

Input artifacts are loaded against their snapshot records. A checksum-valid report must also match the request's configuration/costs, identity/provenance, exact expected evaluation grid, declared engine, candidate path and both comparator paths. Shortened tails or changed headers cannot pass merely by updating a file checksum. The adapter preserves the complete registry snapshot on input/computation failure. Replays return the original recorded result, including failures; the shared diagnostic worker/deadline, 64-candidate limit and existing journal capacity remain in force.

New attempts bind the full current scientific identity as `researchSha256`; universe attempts identify `familyEngine: changing-universe`. These optional metadata fields are omitted when absent so old stored diagnostic JSON shapes and hashes are preserved. Core/Research edits change scientific identity, so frozen studies remain subject to the existing code-change guards. Old plans are not rewritten or reopened. This adapter consumes existing returns, without training, new universe trials, genuine holdout access or broker calls.

The universe return paths still lack FX conversion, raw execution prices and an external market benchmark. The UI and returned limitations state those restrictions. Retrospective family diagnostics do not supply registered changing-universe candidate selection or final evaluation; those remain separate work in [initial v1 acceptance](initial-v1-acceptance.md).

Built-asset browser checks use authored responses: successful universe analysis, both-register disclosure, legacy-model history labels, delayed history selection, 390-pixel layout and zero production research writes.
